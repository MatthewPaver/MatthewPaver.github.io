# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 28 July 2026*

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Matthew Paver builds focused software that helps project and product teams check evidence before they approve a change, send a follow-up or ship an AI output.

**What it does:** The portfolio leads with three public tools. ProjectLens reviews a change pack against its schedule, MeetingProof turns labelled notes into an approved follow-up, and Output Gate checks known AI-output requirements in the browser or CI.

**Product category:** Product engineering portfolio with public software demonstrations.

**Product type:** Static portfolio, browser tools and open-source repositories.

**Business model:** The public software is free to inspect. The portfolio supports hiring, collaboration and future product conversations.

## Target Audience

**Target companies:** Project delivery organisations and product teams that value inspectable workflows.

**Decision-makers:** Engineering managers, product leaders, PMO leaders and delivery leads.

**Primary use case:** Assess Matthew's product and engineering work through a usable workflow, its source and its stated limits.

**Jobs to be done:**

- Review a project change before a board decision.
- Turn meeting notes into an accountable follow-up.
- Catch known AI-output regressions before release.

**Use cases:**

- A hiring manager needs evidence of product judgement and implementation quality.
- A practitioner wants to try a focused workflow without an account.
- A developer wants to inspect or reuse a public implementation.

## Personas

| Persona | Cares about | Challenge | Value we promise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engineering manager | Product judgement, code quality and testing | Portfolios often show screens without a working path | Try the workflow, inspect the source and see the checks |
| Project or operations lead | Clear evidence and human control | Source records disagree when a decision is due | Focused review tools that keep the source visible |
| Developer | Narrow tools that fit existing work | Broad platforms add setup before proving value | Browser examples and public repositories with explicit limits |

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** A polished output can hide missing evidence, conflicting source material or an unreviewed assumption.

**Why alternatives fall short:**

- Generic portfolios list technology without explaining the user's job.
- Broad AI platforms require accounts, traces or model calls for a narrow regression check.
- Summaries and dashboards can separate a claim from the record that supports it.

**What it costs them:** Rework, weak handoffs and decisions made from an incomplete record.

**Emotional tension:** The work looks finished, but the reviewer cannot tell what to trust.

## Competitive Landscape

**Project assurance:** Oracle Primavera Cloud and Deltek Acumen validate demand for schedule diagnostics. ProjectLens focuses on the board decision and the evidence conflicts in front of it.

**Meeting follow-up:** Fellow and Otter already capture transcripts, summaries and actions. MeetingProof focuses on an existing set of notes, exact source lines and approval before export.

**AI evaluation:** LangSmith, Braintrust and Phoenix provide broad evaluation and observability platforms. Output Gate provides a small deterministic check that runs without a model call.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**

- A specific job and finishing point for each flagship tool.
- Source evidence remains visible beside the output.
- Public examples run without an account and use synthetic or public data.
- Product limits appear beside the feature claims.

**How we do it differently:** Each tool narrows the workflow to source material, explicit checks, visible evidence and a human decision.

**Why that is better:** A visitor can understand the job, try it and inspect the implementation without accepting a broad product claim.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| These are demonstrations, not customer products | The site labels demonstrations and synthetic data, then provides the working path and source |
| The projects cover unrelated markets | Three flagship jobs lead the portfolio; components, studies and utilities sit in a supporting section |
| AI produced the interface or copy | The product pages show concrete constraints, test paths, source evidence and known limits |

**Anti-persona:** Someone looking for an autonomous system that replaces professional approval.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** A generic portfolio grid does not explain what any project solves.

**Pull:** Three recognisable work situations offer a clear first click.

**Habit:** Visitors skim screenshots and project names.

**Anxiety:** The public examples may appear more complete than their evidence supports.

## Customer Language

**How they describe the problem:**

- "I just don't get what they are solving."
- "It feels a bit random."
- "There is no user journey."
- "It looks like AI slop."

**Words to use:** change pack, meeting notes, follow-up, source line, requirement, regression, browser, public example, known limit.

**Words to avoid:** marketplace, ecosystem, frontier, revolutionary, intelligent platform, seamless, end-to-end.

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** Direct, calm and technically honest.

**Style:** Name the user, the work in front of them and the record they receive.

**Personality:** Practical, careful, evidence-led and understated.

## Proof Points

**Value themes:**
