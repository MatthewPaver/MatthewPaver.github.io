# Matthew Paver's public portfolio

## Purpose and audience
Help hiring managers and potential collaborators understand Matthew's software, AI and data engineering work, try the public projects and inspect the source. It is a personal portfolio and public project catalogue, not a claim that every project is a commercial product.

## Confirmed direction
Matthew prefers the Tabfolio Pro and Presenta portfolio references, useful app-store browsing, an introduction and CV near the top, real product imagery, video walkthroughs and meaningful motion. He dislikes generic AI copy, decorative metrics, invented screenshots and long pages that obscure each project's purpose.

## Boundaries
Only the existing approved public catalogue belongs here. Do not expose private work or imply adoption, revenue, production deployment or measured business impact without evidence. Keep case studies, reusable templates and finished experiments distinguishable. Keep the public catalogue and existing case routes usable without JavaScript.

## This iteration
The user requested Projecting Success's colour scheme as a reference. Use its navy and cyan relationship for a personal portfolio: navy landing area, cool-white catalogue, high-contrast blue actions and the existing real product media. Do not reuse the company's logo or imply endorsement. Preserve the short personal opening, compact cases, project-specific method and existing routes. Native progressive enhancement, no scroll hijacking and no background video download on load. Motion must be pausable and respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Compact case studies — 5 September 2026
Matthew explicitly chose the current introduction, CV, videos and navy/cyan styling, combined with the prototype's compact case tabs, a shorter evidence-linked Method and the existing full catalogue. This supersedes the scroll-led project chapters and simple-view switch. Show one complete case at a time on every screen size, with real media, direct case actions, keyboard-accessible tabs and bookmarkable project IDs. Without JavaScript and when printing, show all three source cases. Keep the screening room separate: it previews the output; case tabs explain the work.

Method is three short examples: ProjectLens source tracing, PolicyLens explanation checks and the lakehouse's simple baseline. Link to the relevant case evidence and distinguish local extensions from published code. Do not bring back a generic architecture diagram.

Design variance: 6. Motion intensity: 5. Visual density: 5. Preserve existing hero motion; use a brief, reduced-motion-aware transition between case panels.

### Focused polish — 5 September 2026
Preserve the approved composition and real media. Keep About reachable in the mobile navigation, share the MP mark and control geometry between pages, and reduce the mobile catalogue's control height with a native Explore selector. Keep all five exploration modes, URL state and no-JavaScript browsing. The photography credits accordion is removed at Matthew's request; source attribution remains in the asset metadata.

### Public reuse — 5 September 2026
The catalogue must let a visitor choose an immediate useful action: try a browser workflow, run a local example, adapt a developer template or watch a historical case. Show setup requirements and data limitations before the click. Use current real application states as thumbnails, not architecture illustrations pretending to be product screens. First-use instructions are optional and remain usable without JavaScript. Do not equate a polished case study with a safe deployable service or imply that a fixed browser snapshot retrains models.
