PolicyLens

Cloud security

Could a permission change give someone too much access?

A proposed AWS change would let someone delete a production database. PolicyLens shows the line that allows it, checks that a fix removes it without breaking the access people need, and saves a record of the review.

Try it in your browser. It uses a made-up example and nothing is uploaded.

Example
A change that suddenly allows deleting a database
Limits
Checks only the policies you give it, not a whole AWS account

Node.js, AWS IAM, AI evaluation

ProjectLens

Project controls

Does a project update match its schedule?

ProjectLens compares two versions of a project schedule with the written update. It flags dates that moved without explanation, so a reviewer knows what to ask and can record a decision.

Try it in your browser. The example uses a made-up project, so you need no company files.

Example
The finish date moves 73 days while the update says nothing changed
Limits
Shows what changed, not why it changed or who is responsible

Browser JavaScript, Python, Playwright

RAG Regression Gate

AI assistants

After an update, will an AI assistant still find the right policy?

Many workplace assistants look up documents before they answer. This tool compares the documents they find before and after an update, and holds the update if an old, unapproved document now ranks above the current one.

Try it in your browser. Step through six example staff questions, then download the full report.

Example
An old 365-day proposal now ranks above the approved 30-day policy
Limits
Checks which documents are found, not whether the answer is right

Python, search testing, CI

An architect who builds.

Since November 2025 I have been an Automation & AI Consultant at Ciklum, placed at Trustpilot, where I lead AI and automation projects from discovery to support after launch. Before that I spent six years at Projecting Success, progressing from data analysis through data science into solutions architecture. That path still shapes how I work: understand the decision, check the data, build the system and explain it to the people who will use it.

My experience spans knowledge graphs, AI-assisted workflows and cloud automation, alongside release management, privacy reviews and technical training. I’m interested in what happens after a prototype works: who operates it, how it fails and how people stay in control.

These public projects are independent work. QuickSupply came from an outdated Liverpool supply-teacher booking process; Winchester Buyer Check came from my own home search. They show how I work without exposing employer or client systems.
