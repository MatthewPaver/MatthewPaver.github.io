How I work

I start with the decision someone needs to make, then test the part most likely to mislead them. Here is what that looks like in the work.

  1. Keep the source beside the answer.

    In ProjectLens, a reviewer can follow a moved date back to the schedule entries behind it. That helps them ask a better question. It does not tell them who caused the delay.

    Follow the ProjectLens example

  2. Test the explanation, too.

    PolicyLens tests that the AI’s explanation matches the permission result and the policy line it quotes. This explanation check is a local extension; the published demo uses a deterministic permission check.

    Inspect the PolicyLens checks

  3. Compare with the simple baseline.

    In the local lakehouse extension’s next-day forecast, the simple guess is “tomorrow will look like today”. I keep future data out of training and say plainly when the model does no better than that guess.

    Read the lakehouse evaluation

An architect who builds

Since November 2025 I have been an Automation & AI Consultant at Ciklum, placed at Trustpilot. Before that I spent six years at Projecting Success, progressing from data analysis through data science into solutions architecture. That path still shapes how I work: understand the decision, check the data, build the system and explain it to the people who will use it.

My experience spans knowledge graphs, AI-assisted workflows and cloud automation, alongside release management, privacy reviews and technical training. I’m interested in what happens after a prototype works: who operates it, how it fails and how people stay in control.

These public projects are independent work. QuickSupply came from an outdated Liverpool supply-teacher booking process; Winchester Buyer Check came from my own home search. They show how I work without exposing employer or client systems.
