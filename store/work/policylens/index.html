PolicyLens: remove risky access, keep the access people need

A permission change lets someone delete a production database. PolicyLens shows where that access came from and checks a correction that keeps required report access.

Explore PolicyLens Review your own policies Technical evidence Inspect the source

The problem

An engineer reviewing a cloud permission change needs more than a list of edited lines. They need to see what the change allows, whether the risky access is removed and whether the service can still do its job.

The decision it supports

The review connects a concrete consequence to the policy statement responsible. It then checks a proposed correction against the risky request and the access that must survive. A service owner can inspect what was removed, what was retained and the exported review evidence.

Limits

The browser checks supplied policies and declared requests, not whole-account safety or production approval. Service control policies, permission boundaries, session policies and cross-account interactions are excluded. Independent AWS simulator comparison remains outstanding. The public demo has no AI or account-analysis service.

Independent public project by Matthew Paver. Examples are synthetic. No client deployment, adoption, time saving or measured commercial benefit is claimed. User comprehension has not yet been measured.

Complete journey

  1. Open the synthetic permission-change example. Inspect the added database-delete permission and the source statement that grants it.
  2. Check the correction. See the removed deletion statement and retained report statement, then inspect the separate access verification.
  3. Download the review JSON. The original proposal stays distinct from the corrected result.
  4. Choose Use your own policies to open or paste current, proposed and corrected policies with the requests and required access you want checked. Policies stay in the browser.

Why the design changed

Before, visitors faced empty policy editors before understanding the job. After, a working example leads from the risk to its source, a checked correction and an export. The correction view separates removed, retained and added statements from the engine’s access decisions. Retaining a statement alone does not prove effective access is retained.

Technical evidence

The example cites S2 at after.json:10 for rds:DeleteDBInstance. The correction removes that access while retaining the declared s3:GetObject report request. Authored checks include a 55-case benchmark and six captured grounding expectations. Browser journeys cover correction and export, own files, invalid inputs, stale results, missing required access, phones and reduced motion. These checks do not establish parity with AWS or live-model quality.
